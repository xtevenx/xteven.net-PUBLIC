Chilcotins 2025

This was the big trip of the summer. I had wanted to explore the Chilcotins for a while, and finally had enough time to do it. Admittedly, some of the time I forced myself to make because school was burning me out big time...

August 14, 2025

The goal for this day was to make it as far up the Sea to Sky Corridor as possible while keeping it really easy. Minutes after getting on the highway, I picked up a license plate off the shoulder---which I deemed a good omen for the trip---and attached it to my bike. From there until Squamish was all smooth sailing and wonderfully uneventful, especially because I had ridden the S2S before and knew that it was all nice riding apart from the hill after Britannia Beach where the shoulder gets a bit narrow for a few hundred metres.

My first stop was at the McDonald's in Squamish because at this point it's a bit of a tradition for me to stop there every time I pass Squamish on a trip because of how many times I'd done that when I was young. After ordering food, the staff that I was not allowed to have my bike inside, which has happened so many times now that I feel somewhere in McDonald's management that gets set as a rule. Sometimes other restaurants are fine with the bike inside but never McDonald's.

Leaving there, I made a quick stop at the visitor centre to fill up on water before continuing on the paved section of the Sea to Sky Trail which was a good respite from the highway traffic. I decided to skip the next unpaved section of the Sea to Sky Trail because I had heard it gets quite rocky for the climb out of Squamish. Thus, I stayed on the highway, which though paved was still a bit of a slog since it's pretty much an extended climb to Whistler. Never very steep but only with a few rollers it's slow progress all the way. All this time I see the clouds gradually coming in overhead: there was forecast of an atmospheric river event around Squamish.

Eventually, wanting a change of pace from the highway I turn off to join the Sea to Sky trail again, which started off with some fun singletrack but quickly devolved into what seemed to be a service road for BC Hydro infrastructure for the lake that was by the road, which was quite awful to ride on given the quantity of loose rock on the surface. Therefore, when was able to get back on the highway I did until the turnoff for Brandywine Falls, because after that I knew that the Sea to Sky Trail after that would be nicely maintained. And it was, from there to the Cal-Cheak Campground was all quite fun riding and I arrived at the campground just as the rain started.

Quickly unpacking my bags to try to set up before the downpour, I started looking for... my tent stakes and poles which were nowhere to be found. After basically flipping through all my packed gear I concluded that I must have forgot them at home, which was fun (note the heavy sarcasm). Luckily, since I had a trekking pole tent I was able to pitch it quite well with some sticks and stones that I found, but proceeded to hide inside while I searched for anywhere in Whistler where I could buy a pair of trekking poles and some tent stakes. After that whole debacle I was quite tired (though more emotionally than anything) so ate dinner and fell asleep pretty quickly.

August 15, 2025

Listening to the rhythm of the falling rain, I opened my eyes to the shadows of two wooden poles, telling me what a fool I've been. On the bright side, bad weather at the start of a trip often helps me get into a quick morning routine. At least that's what I told myself.

I set off just after 7:00 and reached the heart of Whistler in about an hour. With few restaurants open that early, I just chose whatever food was convenient, which ended up being a burger Splitz Grill. The next stop was the outdoors store just around the corner where I ordered trekking poles and tent stakes at, but upon arrival, it turns out that their system was out of sync and both of these items were unavailable at this location, unlike what was shown online. They were nice, however, and gave me a choice on some alternate models of the items to match my price. After deliberating for quite a while about how many stakes I needed, I was off again at around 10:00 (I bought all six).

At 11:30, I rolled into the Village of Pemberton to get some food for the next few days. I also tried to gather some up to date info on Highline Road, but the visitor centre worker accidentally identified it as the In-SHUCK-ch FSR and gave info for that instead. No matter, I just double checked the DriveBC website and convinced myself it'd be fine, like what I always do.

But it is here where I found myself at a crossroads: either push to cross Highline and hopefully dodge the atmospheric river, or camp the night on this side and wait out the rain. I took a short lay down on a picnic bench to consider the odds, while a motorcycle meetup came and went in the parking lot beside me. Then the rain started to fall. It seems my indecision decided what to do.

I got to Owl Creek Recreation Site right before 15:00, set up camp in the rain, and finished everything for the day by 17:00. The entire day totalled only 60km, but maybe that'll set up me up good for the heavy climbing days in the coming days.

August 16, 2025

I woke up and checked the weather. Rain until midday, then clearing up in the afternoon. Just what I expected. The morning passed slow as I tried to pack up while keeping everything dry, preparing for the first day into the unknown (for me).

A few others seemed to have rolled into the campsite while I was hidden in my tent last night, so I rolled around and said "hi" to some of them while filling up on water. Then, I got going through some intermittent showers.
